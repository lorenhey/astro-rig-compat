Mathematical & Physical Engineering Foundations

AstroRigCompat implements exact optical, physical, and electrical equations used by precision astrophotographers and optical engineers:

1. Filter Refractive Index Light Path Displacement: $$\Delta z = t \cdot \left(\frac{n - 1}{n}\right) \approx \frac{t}{3} \quad (n \approx 1.5)$$ Glass substrate filters (typically $t = 2.0\text{mm}$, refractive index $n \approx 1.5$) push the focal point backward by approximately $+0.67\text{mm}$, requiring the physical spacing to increase accordingly.
2. Geometric Sensor Diagonal & Vignetting Illumination: $$D_{\text{sensor}} = \sqrt{W^2 + H^2}, \qquad R_{\text{illum}} = \frac{\Phi_{\text{circle}}}{D_{\text{sensor}}}$$ When $R_{\text{illum}} < 1.0$, mechanical/optical vignetting is guaranteed at the sensor corners, requiring dark sky flat-field calibration or a smaller sensor crop.
3. Equatorial Mount Payload Capacity Ratio: $$\eta_{\text{mount}} = \frac{\sum m_{\text{payload}}}{C_{\text{rated}}} \times 100\%$$ While visual astronomy allows up to $100\%$ capacity, high-precision astrophotography autoguiding requires $\eta_{\text{mount}} \le 60\% - 65\%$ to avoid periodic error degradation and wind sail oscillation.
4. DC Power Budget & Voltage Drop: $$I_{\text{total, continuous}} = \sum_{k} I_{k, \text{cont}}, \qquad I_{\text{total, peak}} = \sum_{k} I_{k, \text{peak}} \le I_{\text{supply}}$$ DC 12V center-positive distribution lines must not exceed power supply continuous rating to prevent camera TEC cooler brownouts or mount stepper stalls.
